The Real Movers

Who actually shifted to renewable energy, 2014–2024

In 2024, for the first time, wind and solar supplied more of the world's energy than hydropower. Over the decade the world's renewable share rose from 9.7% to 14.8% of primary energy, and almost all of that came from wind and solar. Hydro's share slipped.

That changes who counts as a leader. Iceland, Norway and Canada top most rankings because of rivers and dams built decades ago. This project separates countries that already had renewables from countries that moved.

What changed from the first version

  • Leads with change over the decade, not a 10-year average that hides it.
  • Splits growth by source, so old hydropower is not counted as a transition.
  • Shows the world as a reference line instead of 80 unlabelled lines.
  • Uses one country per dot and per row. The old histogram counted country-years and called them countries.
  • States its coverage gaps instead of hiding them in grey map areas.

Method

  • All energy: renewables as a share of primary energy, using the substitution method. Wind, solar and hydro are counted as the fossil fuel input they would replace, so they are not undercounted against fuels that lose energy as heat.
  • Electricity: renewables as a share of electricity generation. This is the larger number people usually quote.
  • "Other renewables" covers biofuels, bioenergy, geothermal and similar sources.
  • Change is 2024 minus 2014, in percentage points. 2025 electricity data is still incomplete, so it is left out.
